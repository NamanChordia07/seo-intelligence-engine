## Start here: the India legality question applies to both

For Indian residents, the first filter is not spreads but authorisation. RBI states that residents may undertake forex transactions only with authorised persons and on authorised electronic trading platforms or recognised stock exchanges, and that an entity's absence from its Alert List should not be read as authorisation.[^1] RBI also states that residents who remit money for unauthorised forex transactions may face penal action under FEMA.[^2]

Neither broker should therefore be assumed to be permitted for Indian residents unless it appears on RBI's list of authorised platforms.[^2] Our guide on [whether forex trading is legal in India](/hi-in/guides/bharat-mein-forex-trading-legal-hai-kya) covers this in detail.

## Regulation and licences

Third-party directories attribute several licences to Exness group entities, but licence numbers and entity names differ between those sources, so we classify them as conflicting and unverified.[^3] For XM, our research collected no register-level evidence during this run, so we make no licence claims at all.

What you can do: identify the exact legal entity named in your client agreement, then search that entity on the regulator's own register - for example the UK FCA Register[^4] or CySEC's published registers.[^5] A licence held by one group company does not automatically cover an account opened with a different, offshore entity.

## Trading costs: compare the method, not marketing numbers

Advertised "from" spreads rarely match what a retail account pays. The spread is the difference between the bid and ask price, usually measured in pips.[^6] To compare fairly, record the actual spread on the same instrument at the same time on both platforms' live quotes, then add commission and any overnight swap.[^7] Work through the full calculation for your own position size before comparing.

## Leverage and risk

High leverage is often presented as a feature, but it magnifies losses as much as gains. When the EU regulator ESMA restricted CFDs in 2018, it cited national analyses showing that 74-89% of retail CFD accounts typically lost money, and it capped retail leverage at between 30:1 and 2:1 depending on the underlying.[^8] Any leverage figure offered to you should be read with that context in mind.

## Bottom line

On the evidence available, the most important difference for an Indian resident is not between Exness and XM at all - it is between platforms that are authorised for residents and those that are not.
